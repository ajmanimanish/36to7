# 36to7 — Product Description

## 1. Product Summary

**36to7** is a private, web-first relationship companion for people who have met someone they are seriously considering as a life partner.

It is inspired by the Indian **36 Guna / 8 Koota** marriage framework. 36to7 preserves that cultural structure while explicitly presenting the modern meanings as **our interpretation**, translated into practical areas of life that two people can understand, discuss and experience together.

36to7 is **not**:
- a matchmaking marketplace;
- an astrology or horoscope prediction product;
- a compatibility test;
- a 36-question questionnaire;
- an algorithm that tells someone whether they should marry;
- a score that claims to predict marriage success.

Its purpose is to help an intelligent adult answer a more practical question:

> **“What do I need to understand about myself, about this person, and about the life we might build together before I choose?”**

The product provides a framework and an evolving private/shared journey. The users provide the real life.

---

## 2. Product Philosophy

### 2.1 The central idea

> **The user provides the life. 36to7 provides the structure.**

Users meet, talk, call, message, travel, meet families, disagree, learn, reflect and make decisions. 36to7 should fit around that reality rather than manufacture relationship activity.

### 2.2 Alignment is not sameness

36to7 does not assume that two people need identical personalities, interests or preferences.

The product distinguishes between:
- alignment;
- comfortable differences;
- meaningful differences worth discussing;
- unresolved areas;
- serious concerns.

The user's own priorities matter. A difference in a low-importance area should not be treated the same way as a difference in an essential area.

### 2.3 Uncertainty is legitimate

The user is allowed to say:
- I do not know yet;
- I am still figuring this out;
- I have a sense, but I am not sure;
- we have not really explored this.

36to7 should never turn uncertainty into failure.

### 2.4 No forced completion

There is no requirement to complete all 36 Guna.

There is no “Question 17 of 36.”

There is no completion percentage that pressures the user to finish the framework.

The 36 Guna are a **map**, not a task list.

### 2.5 Pointers, not tasks

The 7 Vows are not assignments, courses or checklists.

They are pointers for deeper connection after two people have decided to marry.

## 3. Product Stages

### Stage A — Understanding the framework

The user first understands:
- why 36to7 exists;
- the cultural roots of the 8 Kootas / 36 Guna structure;
- that the modern interpretations are 36to7's own;
- why a broad framework can be useful without replacing personal judgment.

### Stage B — Understanding yourself

The user gradually identifies:
- what matters to them;
- what is essential;
- what is important;
- what is flexible;
- what they are uncertain about.

This does not need to be completed in one sitting.

### Stage C — Getting to know someone

The user creates a journey for a person they are seriously getting to know.

The user can continue their relationship naturally outside the product.

### Stage D — Reflecting

After calls, meetings or conversations, the user can return and capture what stayed with them.

Default interaction:

> **What stayed with you?**

The user can write freely.

Optional prompts:
- I learned something about them;
- something surprised me;
- something felt different from what I expected;
- something changed for me;
- something I am still wondering about;
- something I want to remember.

### Stage E — Understanding evolves

36to7 organizes reflections into the 36 Guna framework and keeps track of an evolving understanding.

The product may surface:
- what is becoming clearer;
- what is still taking shape;
- what appears different;
- what the user is discovering about themselves;
- what may be worth exploring next.

### Stage F — Two-person journey

The partner may join later.

Each person has their own private perspective. Shared content is explicitly controlled.

For important areas, the product can eventually show:
- my view;
- my understanding of them;
- their own view;
- what we understand together.

### Stage G — The decision

Eventually the user can mark the relationship stage as:

> **We have decided to marry.**

That is the transition point to the 7 Vows.

### Stage H — 7 Vows

The 7 Vows are introduced as seven culturally familiar, modern pointers for deepening connection before married life.

They are not additional compatibility tests.

### Stage I — Marriage and beyond

The initial release may stop at the pre-wedding journey. The architecture should, however, leave room for a later married-life product without forcing that scope into V1.

## 4. Cultural Foundation

The product retains the traditional Koota names:

1. Varna
2. Vashya
3. Tara
4. Yoni
5. Graha Maitri
6. Gana
7. Bhakoot
8. Nadi

The total structure remains:

**1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 + 7 + 8 = 36 Guna**

The product must clearly state:

> **36to7 keeps the traditional structure and reinterprets its meaning for modern relationships. The modern lenses are our own interpretation; they are not presented as the original ancient meanings of the Guna.**

Do not claim that the modern lenses are directly prescribed by the Vedas or by the ancient system.

Do not present the 36 point total as a scientifically validated modern marriage predictor.

## 5. The 36 Guna Framework

### VARNA — 1

**1. Core Values & Life Orientation**

What gives your life meaning and what kind of person do you want to become?

Themes include values, ambition, meaning, spirituality, individuality, ethics, growth and traditional/modern outlook.

### VASHYA — 2

**2. Independence & Personal Space**

How much freedom, privacy and individuality does each person need?

**3. Influence & Decision-Making**

How will two independent people make important decisions together?

### TARA — 3

**4. Emotional Support**

How do you want to be supported, understood and comforted?

**5. Stress & Resilience**

How do you respond to pressure, uncertainty and setbacks?

**6. Wellbeing & Self-Care**

How do you look after yourselves and each other?

### YONI — 4

**7. Affection & Touch**

How do you experience physical affection and closeness?

**8. Sexual Desire & Expectations**

What place does sexual intimacy have in the relationship?

**9. Sexual Communication & Boundaries**

How comfortably can you talk about intimacy, preferences, boundaries and consent?

**10. Reproductive & Intimate Health**

What should each person understand about intimate and reproductive expectations?

### GRAHA MAITRI — 5

**11. Friendship & Companionship**

Do you enjoy being together beyond romance?

**12. Communication**

How do you express yourselves and understand each other?

**13. Emotional Connection**

How do you experience closeness and emotional intimacy?

**14. Trust & Reliability**

Can you depend on each other, and do words and actions generally align?

**15. Shared Enjoyment**

What makes ordinary life enjoyable together?

### GANA — 6

**16. Temperament**

How are you naturally wired?

**17. Everyday Rhythm**

How do routines, structure and daily habits fit together?

**18. Social Energy**

How much people, socialising and community does each person want around them?

**19. Lifestyle & Interests**

How do you like to spend life: travel, food, music, books, sports, hobbies, pets, entertainment and more?

**20. Conflict Behaviour**

What happens when you disagree?

**21. Adaptability & Difference**

How comfortably can you live with differences and change?

### BHAKOOT — 7

**22. Life Vision**

What kind of life are you each trying to build?

**23. Career & Work**

What role will work, ambition, entrepreneurship and career play in your lives?

**24. Money & Financial Life**

How do you think about earning, spending, saving, investing, independence and sharing?

**25. Home & Geography**

Where and how do you imagine living?

**26. Family & Parents**

How will families fit into your life together?

**27. Children & Parenthood**

Do you want children, and how do you imagine parenthood?

**28. Culture, Religion & Shared Life**

What beliefs, traditions and cultural practices will shape your home?

### NADI — 8

**29. Health & Disclosure**

What should you understand about each other's health and wellbeing?

**30. Family Health & Hereditary Context**

Are there family health considerations that may be relevant to your future together?

**31. Reproductive Health & Fertility**

What are your expectations around reproductive health and fertility?

**32. Parenthood Resilience**

How might you face unexpected challenges around becoming or raising a family?

**33. Long-Term Care**

How would you want to care for each other through illness, disability or ageing?

**34. Family Responsibility Over Time**

What responsibilities toward parents or dependants might shape the future?

**35. Sustainable Life**

Can the life you are imagining remain workable as circumstances change?

**36. Growing Old Together**

What do you imagine your life looking like many years from now?

## 6. Personal Priority Model

Each Guna or topic can have a user-specific importance state:

- **Essential**
- **Important**
- **Flexible**
- **Not sure yet**

This is the user's own framework weighting.

The traditional 1–8 Koota weighting must not be treated as a modern scientific weighting of relationship importance.

The user can change priorities at any time.

---

## 7. Understanding Model

The system should conceptually distinguish:

### My view
What I want, believe or expect.

### My understanding of them
What I currently think their position is.

### Their view
What they independently say or record.

### Our understanding
What the two people have explicitly discussed and understand together.

### Confidence
How clear the user feels, without turning this into a visible “score.”

Suggested internal states:
- guessing;
- some sense;
- pretty sure;
- clear.

User-facing wording can remain more natural and contextual.

---

## 8. Reflection Model

The default reflection interaction is free-form.

The product should preserve the user's own words and allow an optional AI-generated summary.

AI can extract:
- relevant Guna;
- what seems clearer;
- what remains open;
- potential differences;
- changes in the user's own thinking;
- a possible next area worth discovering.

AI must stay close to the content provided and avoid inventing certainty.

---

## 9. Relationship States

Suggested user-facing state language:

- **Not explored**
- **Taking shape**
- **Understood**
- **Aligned**
- **Different but okay**
- **Worth discussing**
- **Important unresolved**

Internal models can be more granular; the UI should remain simple.

---

## 10. Serious Concerns

A separate layer may flag matters that deserve attention. It is not part of the 36 Guna “score.”

Example category:

### Things Worth Pausing On

- Explore
- Important
- Serious concern
- Safety concern

Potential serious areas may include coercion, sexual pressure, repeated boundary violations, severe deception, serious substance misuse, serious financial concealment, degradation/humiliation or violence.

The system must not diagnose a person or tell someone to leave based solely on an automated classification.

Suggested language:

> **Some of what you've described may be a serious relationship concern. Consider discussing this privately with someone you trust or a qualified professional before proceeding.**

---

## 11. The 7 Vows

The Vows become available once the user/couple has explicitly reached the stage:

> **We have decided to marry.**

They are pointers, not tasks.

### 1. The First Honest Conversation
Say the things that matter, especially the things that have been postponed or assumed.

### 2. Meeting the Families
Understand the people, relationships, traditions and responsibilities you're becoming part of.

### 3. Navigating a Real Disagreement
Learn how you find your way back to each other when you do not see things the same way.

### 4. Sharing a Fear Out Loud
Let each other see the parts of you that may need understanding, patience or support.

### 5. Talking About the Next Ten Years
Imagine the life you are actually going to build, not only the wedding ahead.

### 6. Weathering a Hard Season
Talk about how you want to stand together when life does not go according to plan.

### 7. Choosing Each Other, Plainly
After everything you've discovered, consciously choose the person you're building a life with.

## 12. Website Product Scope

The first implementation is **web-first**.

It should be a responsive website/web application that works well on desktop and mobile browsers. A native mobile application is not required for V1.

The web product should include:

- public marketing site;
- educational framework pages;
- user account creation/login;
- personal profile;
- one or more relationship journeys;
- 36 Guna exploration;
- priorities;
- reflection journal;
- evolving understanding;
- shared/private controls;
- optional partner invite;
- relationship-stage transition;
- 7 Vows;
- settings/privacy/export/delete;
- AI-assisted organization of reflections;
- basic notifications/email where useful.

---

## 13. Success Criteria for V1

The first release is successful if real users demonstrate that:

1. They understand the cultural/practical proposition quickly.
2. The 36 Guna feel comprehensive without feeling like a questionnaire.
3. Users naturally return after real interactions.
4. Reflection is easier and more useful than structured form-filling.
5. AI organization helps users see meaningful things they had already experienced or written down.
6. Users understand where they are aligned and where they remain uncertain without needing a compatibility score.
7. At least some users voluntarily invite a partner.
8. Users find the 7 Vows relevant once they have decided to marry.
9. Privacy controls feel trustworthy enough for sensitive material.

Real-user feedback should be treated as the primary stress test for taxonomy and UX.
